Job Market Paper

Mean and Variance Regimes in Threshold VARs: Revisiting Regime-dependent Fiscal Multipliers

Do the effects of fiscal policy depend on the state of the economy? Empirical studies disagree, finding larger multipliers in recessions, in expansions, or no difference at all. This paper traces part of the disagreement to how the regimes are defined, and develops threshold VAR estimators that let the data define them. We provide the estimation theory for two models with regime-dependent volatility: a joint model, in which variance breaks sharpen the identification of the threshold, and a decoupled model, in which mean and variance regimes are driven by different observable state variables. We establish consistency, derive the nonstandard limiting distributions of the thresholds, and show that their super-consistent rate leaves inference on the regime parameters unaffected. Applied to U.S. government spending multipliers, the framework estimates the regimes rather than imposing them and separates the business-cycle state from the shock environment: wars and large fiscal events form a variance regime of their own. Once this environment is set apart, significant state dependence emerges and post-WWII multipliers are larger in expansions than in recessions, reconciling the conflicting findings of the literature. A robustness exercise under an alternative identification scheme validates the estimated regimes and the direction of state dependence.
